Consilium: the federated context layer for AI agents in medical research.

Agents run inside the institution's own environment; clinical and genomic data never leaves. Every decision — conclusion, evidence, rejected alternatives, who signed off — is written to an append-only chain that verifies offline.

The same record lets one institution's agents query another's context without moving a patient record, and attributes every contribution — so shared context is credited and compensated, not given away.

Capabilities

What Consilium provides

In-environment execution

Agents run in the institution's VPC or on-premises. Clinical and genomic data stays inside the network boundary; no PHI egresses at any point.

deployed in your infrastructure

Decision recording

Every agent action is written to an append-only log: the conclusion, the evidence used, the alternatives rejected, and the reviewer who signed off.

append-only · sha-256 chained

Independent verification

The chain verifies offline on the institution's own hardware, with no call to our servers. Canonicalization follows RFC 8785 in two byte-exact implementations.

rfc 8785 · open source

Scoped context retrieval

Institutional knowledge is structured on the open aDNA standard, so an agent loads the relevant subgraph instead of a corpus. Fewer tokens per query, and the right ones.

adna · mit licensed

Calibrated abstention

Where evidence is insufficient the system declines to answer and names what is missing. Abstentions are recorded alongside results and can be measured.

recorded, not suppressed

Federated dispatch

Workloads reach external HPC or cloud GPUs, and questions reach peer institutions. A specification or query crosses; records do not.

optional · off by default

Use cases

What researchers do with it

Score a gene's variants

Run protein language models across every variant in a gene, cross-referenced to ClinVar, gnomAD and the literature, returning a ranked list with the evidence behind each call. Benchmarked at 14,298 classified variants across 156 genes in 431 s, median AUC 0.938.

Find patients no code captures

Identify candidates from unstructured record text, including undiagnosed patients who carry no diagnostic code and do not appear in code-based queries.

Assemble an evidence package

Gather what is known about a target, variant or care pathway across scattered sources, each assertion carrying the document and sentence it came from.

Reach a supercomputer

Dispatch molecular simulation or large-scale scoring to an HPC allocation while patient data remains behind the institutional firewall.

Ask a peer institution

Put a cohort or phenotype question to another node and receive counts and summaries — or a recorded abstention where coverage is absent.

Answer a review board

Produce the decision history for any result: what ran, what it concluded, what it rejected, who reviewed it, and proof the record was not altered.

Why now

Who holds the record of what AI decided in medicine is being settled in the next two years.

Every AI decision in medicine will be logged somewhere. The question is whose log: a cloud provider's, rented and editable — or the institution's own, append-only, verifiable offline and readable by a peer. The first deployments decide, and they are happening now.

Agents can read a local schema

A frontier model answers a semantic question against an unmapped local schema. Mapping to OMOP, i2b2 or PCORnet before the first query — what stalled federated networks for a decade — becomes a query-time step. Feasible since 2025.

Our thesis · stated as such

Record-keeping became a legal duty

The EU AI Act requires automatic logging for high-risk AI systems, phasing in from 2026, medical devices following. A record no vendor can quietly edit becomes the requirement.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 · Article 12

The buyer named the bottleneck

In February 2026 the World Economic Forum's Making Rare Diseases Count named federated data sharing across health systems a priority strategy for a $7.2–8.6 trillion-a-year problem.

WEF · February 2026

Biology is computable. Care is not.

Mondo, HPO and Monarch made rare-disease biology computable. Nothing equivalent exists for care — what to do, where, with which resources. The layer is unbuilt.

Mondo · HPO · Monarch

An institution cannot own its record if a vendor owns the format. So the format is open — published schema, RFC 8785, MIT verifier — and we sell the deployment and the network.

The network

Institutions can query each other without moving records.

Rare-disease knowledge sits in institutions that cannot exchange records. A verifiable record on both ends lets a question travel where data cannot: one node asks, nodes that can answer return counts and summaries, nodes that cannot record an abstention. Only the question and the answer cross.

Context, not data

What crosses is a question and a provenance-carrying answer. Records stay where they are, under the governance they are already under.

Abstention is an answer

A node with no coverage says so, and that is recorded. Silence becomes data — it marks where the gaps are.

Attribution is built in

The record states which institution contributed what to which result. Credit for contributed context is computable rather than negotiated.

Access

If a question travels instead of data, joining costs almost nothing.

Federated networks have been limited to institutions that could afford to join: data engineering, common-model mapping, legal work to move records.

Here a participant asks without contributing a dataset, building a pipeline or transferring anything. A regional clinic, a remote hub, a patient organisation or a low-resource health system can join on the asking side alone.

Access is a policy setting

Participation is configured, not integrated. Pro-bono or humanitarian access is a change to a participant's authorization scope, not a separate product or contract.

Answers carry their assumptions

Guidance records the resource tier and jurisdiction it was written under, and what substitutes when a recommended service is out of reach. A tertiary-centre answer is not handed unqualified to a remote clinic.

Asking and contributing are separate

A participant that only asks still gets answers. One that contributes context is attributed for it. Neither requires the other.

This is why the company is a public benefit corporation: cheap to answer and cheap to join is what makes pro-bono access practical, and the mission is in the charter so it survives commercial pressure.

Founding cohort

The first institutions set the rules the network runs on.

The network is being assembled now and little about it is fixed: the context schema, the query semantics, the governance model, what an agent may ask of another institution. The first institutions decide these with us, in the deployment — they are the Consilium network.

A partnership begins as a single-institution deployment that stands on its own. Cross-institution querying switches on separately, on the institution's terms, with peers it selects.

FAQ

Questions

Does any patient data leave the environment?

No. Models execute inside the institution's environment and PHI does not egress. When a workload reaches external compute or a peer asks a question, what crosses is a specification or query and a de-identified answer — each crossing written to the chain.

We already have Bedrock or Azure OpenAI under a BAA. Why not just use that?

Consilium runs on top of them. A hyperscaler provides model inference inside a compliance boundary; it does not provide variant interpretation, phenotype extraction, evidence synthesis or agent orchestration, and it does not record what an agent concluded, which evidence it used, what it rejected, or who signed off. Cloud audit logs record that an API was called, not what was decided.

What does aDNA do about inference cost?

aDNA is an MIT-licensed open standard for structuring knowledge so agents can navigate it. Rather than retrieving bulk documents into context, an agent resolves to a scoped subgraph — the nodes relevant to the question — so fewer tokens are spent per query and the tokens spent carry relevant context. The structure is open, so the institution can inspect and extend it.

Could a platform team build this in-house?

The single-institution version is a feasible build — on the order of six to twelve months for a small team, plus maintenance. The record is the component with the least tolerance for error, since canonicalization, chain construction and independent verification are what a review board examines most closely. The record layer is open source and can be adopted directly. A single-institution build does not produce a node other institutions can query.

What does joining the network commit an institution to?

Nothing beyond what is agreed in writing, per query type. The institution chooses which peers may ask, what categories of question they may ask, and what an answer may contain. Cross-institution querying stays off until it is turned on, and a single-institution deployment is a complete deployment.

What happens to the records if the engagement ends?

They remain the institution's. The chain exports in an open format and stays verifiable using tools that do not contact our servers.

Is this a medical device?

No. It is decision support with a clinician in the loop. It is not FDA or CE cleared and is not marketed for clinical diagnosis.

How is it priced?

Founding-cohort terms are set per deployment and discussed on the first call. Pricing follows seats and workload volume, not patient volume or records held.
